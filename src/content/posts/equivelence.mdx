# Terms
Some basic ideas that are mentioned profusely in the logical equivelence chapter is the following. 
I'll include the symbols usually used to represent these, and everytime its symbolized, you can hover to see the meaning. 

**Tautologies (<abbr data-def="a compound proposition that is true under every possible assignment of truth values" tabindex="0">⊤</abbr>)**: A Tautologie is a compound proposition (refer to propositions article, simply put, it combines one or more simple propositions using a logical connective (AND, OR, etc)) that no matter the truth values given as an input, no matter the expected result given those values, the conclusion will always be true. 

**Contradicition (<abbr data-def="a compound proposition that is false under every possible assignment of truth values" tabindex="0">⊥</abbr>)**: pretty much ¬Tautologie (Not Tautologie). Its the same sort of thing but the conclusion will always be false, instead of true. 

**Logically Equivelent**: When multiple compound proposition have identical truth values, they are considiered this. (Ex: r <abbr data-def="two compound propositions with identical truth values in every row" tabindex="0">≡</abbr> s (r is logically equivelent to s))
