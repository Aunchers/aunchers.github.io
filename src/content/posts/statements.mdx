As I was writing the third article in this discrete series, I noticed in the last article I had failed to mention an integral piece of basic discrete. That being, statements and statment symbols. This will be relatively short, but nonetheless important. 

## Terms
**<abbr data-def="read as 'if p, then q'; false only when p is true and q is false" tabindex="0">Conditional Operation</abbr> ($p \to q$)**: Read as *"if p, then q"*.

| $p$ | $q$ | $p \to q$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 0 | 0 | 1 |

If you're like me, then this table may seem confusing. To get a better idea of it, think of it as a **promise**: 
> *"If you clean your room (p), you get ice cream (q)."*
* This promise is **only False (or broken)** when you clean your room (p = 1 [or true]), but don't get ice cream (q = 0 [or false]). 
* If you **don't** clean your room but still get ice cream, the promise wasn't broken (it never forbade getting ice cream other ways).
* If you **don't** clean your room and **don't** get ice cream, the promise was still not broken.
In logic, if the promise isn't broken, the statement is considered **True (1)**. broken.
